# Writing Style Guide, Dunelock (iGEM 2026)

This file defines the voice for all web copy on this project. Read it before
writing or editing any user-facing text, and follow it exactly. Do not deviate
even if a section feels short or informal.

## Core principle

The style is direct, technical and philosophical. It is not marketing copy.
Write the way an engineer or a physicist explains something they actually
understand, not the way a product page sells it.

Ground an abstract idea in something concrete first: a scene, a fact, a moment,
before stating the point. Never open with the abstraction itself.

Take the tone from Feynman's lectures. Simple, clear, and still technical. He
never softened a mechanism to make it friendly. He picked the one thing that
made it obvious and started there.

## Hard rules

These are not preferences. A sentence that breaks one of them gets rewritten.

- **No adjectives that carry no information.** "Powerful", "seamless",
  "innovative", "cutting-edge", "robust" are banned outright. If a word could be
  swapped into any other tech website unchanged, cut it.
- **No filler qualifiers.** "Really", "very", "simply", "just", "in order to".
  Say the thing directly.
- **No unstated assumptions about the reader.** Do not write "you're tired of X"
  or "you need Y". State what the thing is and what it does. The reader decides
  whether they need it.
- **Short declarative sentences by default.** A longer sentence is allowed when a
  real idea needs the extra clause, never for rhythm.
- **Technical terms stay technical.** Do not swap jargon out for a vague word.
  Define it briefly in plain language and keep using it.
- **Philosophical means stepping back to say why something is true**, or what it
  implies. It does not mean decorating sentences with abstraction. One grounded
  observation beats three vague ones.
- **No em dashes and no semicolons.** Use a period, a comma, "and", "but", "so".
- **No rhetorical questions as headers or hooks.** "Ever wondered how..." is out.
- **No AI-sounding structure.** No "unlock", "elevate", "empower". No group of
  three for its own sake. No summary sentence restating what was just said.
- **Mechanism over benefit.** When explaining what something does, describe how
  it works. If the mechanism is clear the value is obvious, so do not assert it
  separately.
- **No hedging.** "Somewhat", "fairly", "quite", "in many ways". State it or
  drop it.
- **No filler transitions.** "It's important to note", "at the end of the day",
  "when it comes to". Start the sentence on the content.
- **Concrete nouns and specific numbers.** "7 students", not "several students".
  A year, not "in the past".

## Structural pattern

1. Open with a scene, image, quote, or specific fact. Not a thesis statement and
   not a definition.
2. Let the scene breathe for a few sentences before connecting it to the
   technical point.
3. State facts plainly, including difficult ones. Do not add emotional
   commentary the facts do not need. Let the fact carry its own weight.
4. Land the point only after the opening has earned it. The reader should feel
   the connection, not be told it ("this shows that...").
5. A question or a quote can open a section, but only if it is answered or
   complicated later. Never left as decoration.

## Technical writing specifically

- Explain mechanisms plainly. If something works a certain way, say how. Do not
  assert that it works well.
- Being humanistic in a technical section means grounding a claim in why it
  matters to a person or a real situation. It does not mean adding emotional
  adjectives to technical sentences.
- Numbers, units, equations, identifiers and citations are not prose. When
  rewriting a paragraph for style, they survive unchanged.

## What to avoid

- Opening with "In today's world..." or any generic framing sentence
- Adjective stacking ("innovative, cutting-edge, powerful solution")
- Explaining the point right after making it ("This demonstrates that...")
- Uniform sentence rhythm, every sentence the same length and shape
- Corporate or startup-pitch tone anywhere on the site

## Reference example

"Rigour is to the mathematician what morality is to men." Who is the man here?

Petrichor. Rain patters the windows of the bus. Conversations loom amidst the
strong noise of rocks scraping. Darkness persists outside. Around 10 exceptional
students in mathematics, mostly women, are present aboard an university bus.
Returning from a conference for gifted individuals in mathematics, a bright
future in this subject awaits them. As the bus transports the students back to
their alma mater, the Sharif University for Technology in Tehran, the winds get
stronger, and whooshing fills the bus. Low light and heavy rains lead to a drop
in visibility. A screeching sound echoes through the bus, and the students go
silent. Moments later, the unprecedented happens, the bus, unable to steer
properly, veers off into a cliff. Sadly, 7 talented students lost their lives in
this horrific accident, among 2 wounded who carry with them the guilt of
survival (Movasati).

Among the survivors was Maryam Mirzakhani, hailed as one of the most exceptional
students of mathematics that Iran has ever produced, becoming the first Iranian
woman to win the Gold Medal at the International Mathematics Olympiad 1994,
winning again with a perfect score in 1995.

Match the rhythm and restraint of this passage: scene first, facts stated without
embellishment, the point arriving late and earned.

## Before finalizing any section

Reread it against this file and cut anything that does not pass. Two checks:

1. Does it open with a scene or a fact, or does it open with a claim? If it opens
   with a claim, rewrite the opening.
2. Would any sentence fit unchanged on a generic startup landing page? If so,
   rewrite it to state the mechanism instead.
