WIND PATTERN MODEL FOR THE BUSINESS CASE
How the analysis was actually done, in plain terms

Written for anyone on the team who wants to understand or defend the wind
side of the business model without reading the code or the papers.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE ONE PARAGRAPH VERSION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The business idea is: treat the loose ground the sand comes from, instead of
cleaning the asset the sand lands on. To charge money for that, we have to be
able to say how much sand arrives at a customer's site now, and how much less
would arrive after we treat the ground upwind. The wind model is the thing that
turns "it is windy in the Gulf" into that number.

It works in five steps: describe the wind properly, work out when the ground
starts moving, work out how much sand the wind carries, work out how far that
sand actually travels, and then convert the sand that arrives into money.

Every step is taken from a published paper. Nothing in the chain is a guess.
Where we could not find a source for a number, the code refuses to run rather
than filling in something plausible. That is deliberate, and it is the part a
judge or a customer will check.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROBLEM WITH JUST USING "AVERAGE WIND SPEED"
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the single most important idea in the whole model, so it goes first.

Sand does not move at all until the wind passes a threshold. Below that speed,
nothing happens. Above it, the amount of sand carried goes up roughly with the
CUBE of the wind speed. Double the wind, and you get about eight times the sand.

That has an awkward consequence. If you take a month of wind data, average it,
and put the average into the sand equation, you get the wrong answer, and not
by a little. A month that is calm for 28 days and screaming for 2 days has a
gentle average, but nearly all of its sand moves on those 2 days. The average
wind hides exactly the thing that matters.

So we never feed an average wind speed into the model. Instead we describe the
wind as a distribution: not "the wind is 6 m/s", but "here is the full spread of
how often the wind is 2, 4, 8, 14 m/s". The standard way to write that spread
down is called a Weibull distribution, and it has just two numbers in it:

  A  the scale, roughly how windy the place is
  k  the shape, roughly how gusty versus steady it is

Then we do the sand calculation across the whole spread and add it up, weighting
each wind speed by how often it actually blows. The result is a genuine monthly
average of the sand moved, not the sand moved by an average wind.

The nice part: this integral can be solved exactly on paper. We did not have to
approximate it or invent a "gustiness fudge factor", which is what a lot of
published work does. The exact answer is in windStats.ts and wind_stats.py, and
scripts/verify_weibull_flux.py checks the formula against a brute-force
numerical version. They agree to about twelve decimal places.

Where the two Weibull numbers come from:
  - ERA5 (the European weather reanalysis) has hourly wind for every point on
    Earth going back decades. Fitting A and k to it, month by month, is what
    gives the seasonal picture.
  - The Global Wind Atlas already publishes fitted A and k at about 250 m
    resolution, which is far finer than ERA5. But we checked, and it is
    ANNUAL ONLY, with no monthly layers and no direction layers. So it can give
    us the map detail but not the season. ERA5 is not optional.

That check saved us from building the seasonal module on a dataset that
physically cannot carry a season.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHEN DOES THE GROUND START MOVING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The threshold from step 1 is not a universal number. It depends on how big the
sand grains are. Fine sand and coarse sand start moving at different wind
speeds. So we needed real grain sizes for our region.

PAPER: Benaafi et al., Arabian Journal of Geosciences, Table 1
       (doi 10.1007/s12517-015-1970-9)

They measured dune sand at ten locations across Saudi Arabia and reported the
grain size statistics for each. We took the three that are the closest match to
UAE dunes, including Rub' al Khali, which crosses into the UAE, and Sakaka. The
paper itself states that eastern UAE dune sands resemble these, so the transfer
is the authors' claim, not ours. That is written into the provenance block of
public/data/uae_parameters.json so nobody later mistakes it for our own
measurement.

From those grain sizes, a standard equation gives the threshold wind speed.

PAPER: Tian et al., Land Degradation and Development 2018
       (doi 10.1002/ldr.3176)

This is the independent check. They ran untreated aeolian sand in a wind tunnel
and measured the speed at which it started moving: 5.73 m/s, at a height of
0.6 m. We ran our equation on our grain sizes and predicted 6.5 to 8.3 m/s at
the same height. Same ballpark, from a completely different direction. That is
what makes the threshold defensible rather than assumed.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHICH WAY DOES THE SAND GO
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Knowing how much sand moves is only half of it. For a business case you need to
know whether it moves TOWARD the customer's site or away from it. A treatable
patch that is downwind of the solar farm is worth nothing.

PAPER: Fryberger (1979), "Dune forms and wind regime", chapter 5 of
       A Study of Global Sand Seas, USGS Professional Paper 1052

This is the standard method in the field for turning a wind rose into a
direction and a strength of sand movement. It gives four numbers:

  DP   drift potential, total sand-moving power of the wind regime
  RDP  the same thing after cancelling out opposing winds
  RDD  the resultant direction, which is the way sand actually ends up going
  UDI  RDP divided by DP, from 0 to 1, how one-directional the wind is

UDI matters commercially. A site with UDI near 1 has a clear dominant wind, so
there is one obvious patch of ground to treat. A site with UDI near 0 has winds
that cancel each other out, and the sand mostly sloshes back and forth instead
of arriving anywhere. Those are much worse customers, and the model says so.

PAPER: Khalaf and Al-Ajmi (1993), Geomorphology 6, 111-134
       (references/kuwait_sandenroachment.pdf)

Used for three separate things:

  1. The Fryberger working equation, Q = V squared times (V minus Vt) times t,
     is quoted in their text. Fryberger's own chapter presents it as tables, so
     this is where the usable formula came from.
  2. The Gulf threshold. They report that saltation in Kuwait starts "whenever
     wind speed reaches 5.4 m/s" at 10 m. That is a measured local number for
     our region, not a textbook global one.
  3. A validation target. They measured about 20 cubic metres of sand drift per
     metre of width per year in Kuwait, mostly May to August, heading southeast.
     Our model has to land near that, and the season and direction they report
     are the Shamal, which is the same wind system that drives the UAE.

In the app, the RDD arrow on the map is compared against the compass bearing
from the nearest mapped sand source to the selected site. If the sand is not
heading toward the site, the tool says so instead of quietly showing a number.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FINDING THAT CHANGED THE BUSINESS CASE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the part that is most worth understanding, because it changed what we
are allowed to claim.

Blown material splits into three behaviours by grain size:

  bigger than 2 mm            creep, rolls along the ground, goes nowhere
  60 micrometres to 2 mm      SALTATION, hops along in a layer about 1 m high
  smaller than 60 micrometres SUSPENSION, floats away, can cross countries

PAPER: Marticorena and Bergametti (1995), doi 10.1029/95JD00690

That is where those size bands and the 1 m layer height come from. It also makes
a further point that turned out to be decisive: the fine suspended dust is
PRODUCED by the saltating grains hammering the surface, and how much gets
produced depends on how much clay is in the soil. Clean quartz sand is a strong
sand mover and a weak dust producer. Clay-rich flood plain is the opposite.

Now apply that to our measured grain sizes from Benaafi. For Rub' al Khali dune
sand, 99.65 percent of the mass is in the saltation band, and only 0.13 percent
is fine enough to suspend. Almost none of it is the floaty kind.

PAPER: Ferguson and Church (2004), J. Sediment. Res. 74, 933-937

Their settling velocity formula tells you how fast a grain of a given size falls
through air. Release our real grain sizes from the 1 m saltation layer, let the
wind push them sideways while gravity pulls them down, and see how far they get.
The answer, computed in scripts/transport_model.py:

  wind        1 m     5 m     10 m    25 m    50 m    100 m   1 km
  5 m/s       96%     7.6%    1.1%    0%      0%      0%      0%
  10 m/s      100%    35%     7.6%    0.5%    0%      0%      0%
  15 m/s      100%    66%     20%     1.9%    0.1%    0%      0%

Read that as "percent of the sand still in the air after travelling this far".
Essentially all the mass we are dealing with lands within tens of metres.

Three things follow immediately:

  a) We do not need an atmospheric dispersion model. No HYSPLIT, no FLEXPART.
     Those tools answer a long-range question that our material does not pose.
     That saved a large amount of work, and it is defensible rather than lazy.

  b) A contradiction in the literature resolved itself. Hennen (2017) tracked
     27,000 individual dust emission events by satellite and found 37 percent of
     Middle East dust starting on the Tigris and Euphrates flood plain, with
     very little from the southern Arabian Peninsula. That looked like bad news
     for a project based in a sand sea. It is not a contradiction. Hennen is
     measuring SUSPENSION dust, which comes from clay. We move SALTATING sand,
     which comes from dunes. Both are true at once, and the grain size physics
     and the satellite observations agree from two independent directions.

  c) The scale mismatch. Every UAE solar asset has a mapped regional dust source
     nearby, 7 to 15 km away for the big ones. We measured those distances from
     Ginoux et al. (2012), Reviews of Geophysics, against real asset locations
     pulled from OpenStreetMap. But Ginoux's grid squares are about 11 km across
     and saltating sand lands within tens of metres. Those two scales differ by
     a factor of roughly a thousand. So a regional hotspot 7 km upwind sends a
     solar farm no saltating sand at all. The sand that abrades and buries a
     solar farm comes from the ground AT the farm and immediately upwind of it.

     This is written into the UI as a hard rule: a user must never be allowed to
     read a regional hotspot as the source of the sand hitting their panels.
     That is the one way this tool could mislead a customer or a judge.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TURNING SAND INTO MONEY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

PAPER: Elminir et al. (2006), Energy Conversion and Management 47, 3192
       (doi 10.1016/j.enconman.2006.02.014)

They measured how much light a solar panel loses as dust builds up on the glass,
and published a curve of transmittance loss against grams of dust per square
metre, plus a table of how the loss changes with panel tilt angle. We read both
off the actual PDF rather than trusting a text extraction, because the equation
matters. That is what converts deposited sand into lost electricity, and lost
electricity into lost revenue.

The full money chain is:

  sand arriving at the site (kg per square metre per year)
    -> dust settled on the glass (grams per square metre)
    -> transmittance loss (percent)          Elminir
    -> energy loss (MWh per year)            capacity factor and nameplate MW
    -> revenue loss (dollars per year)       electricity tariff
    -> product benefit                       revenue loss times our reduction
    -> net value                             benefit minus treatment cost

The treatment cost side was already built in economic.ts, so only the loss side
was new.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHERE OUR PRODUCT ENTERS, AND WHY IT IS ONE NUMBER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the cleanest part of the whole model and it is worth saying out loud in
any pitch.

The wet lab measures ONE thing: the extra cohesion the bacterial crust adds to
the sand, called gamma. That single number raises the threshold wind speed. The
raised threshold moves the lower limit of the integral in step 1. The integral
gets smaller. Less sand moves.

So the entire product story is one measured parameter moving through one
equation. We run the same calculation twice, once with the untreated threshold
and once with the treated threshold, and the difference between the two answers
is what the customer is paying for.

Three MICP papers (Tian 2018, Zomorodian 2019, and a 2022 study in JRMGE that
tested calcareous desert sand in a wind tunnel at 10, 20 and 30 m/s) bracket the
range that gamma could plausibly fall into. They tell a judge our number is
believable. They cannot supply it. The headline efficiency number stays ours,
measured in our own lab, and labelled as such.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR WHICH MARKET WE LEAD WITH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The analysis pushed back on the business plan, and this is the most useful thing
it produced.

The original plan led with solar panel soiling. The physics says local sand
stabilisation does not do much for soiling. Soiling is caused by fine dust that
settles and sticks, and our dune sand is 0.13 percent fine material. Treating a
dune patch removes almost no soiling-capable material, because there was almost
none there to start with. The dust that soils UAE panels comes from far away,
over clay-rich ground we are not treating.

So the three value mechanisms, ranked by how well the evidence supports them:

  1. SAND ENCROACHMENT AND BURIAL. Saltating mass piling against panel rows,
     access roads and perimeter fences. Directly proportional to the mass the
     model computes, at exactly the right length scale. Strongest claim.

  2. ABRASION of glass and equipment. Real, documented, right length scale. But
     we could not find any paper linking saltation flux to glass haze. This is
     the weakest link in the value chain and we say so.

  3. SOILING. Only defensible for treated patches that are themselves clay-rich
     enough to emit fine dust, which dune sand is not. Do not lead with it.

And a genuine strategic conclusion: ROADS may be the better lead market on
physics alone. Sand on a highway is exactly near-field saltation deposition, the
receptor is a line lying directly downwind of treatable shoulders, and the
mechanism needs no extra conversion step at all. The blocker on roads is cost
data, not physics.

The honest claim we can defend is: we reduce local sand arrival and the abrasion
and burial it causes, plus the dust generated on the patches we treat. Anyone
promising to end panel soiling is describing a different product.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT IS STILL MISSING, ON PURPOSE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The code keeps a list of parameters it does not have a source for, and it raises
an error rather than inventing them. Currently:

  - UAE electricity tariff (needed to turn lost energy into dollars)
  - UAE utility PV capacity factor (needed to turn MW into MWh per year)
  - Saltation flux to glass abrasion (no source found; may not exist; a
    candidate for our own bench work)
  - Cost per cubic metre of sand clearing in the Gulf (business team primary
    research; municipalities do it constantly but do not publish the cost)
  - Retention fraction on tilted glass (how much of what lands actually sticks)

Leaving these visibly blank is a feature. A pricing model built on five invented
constants falls apart the moment somebody asks where a number came from.
